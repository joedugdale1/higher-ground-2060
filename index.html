I.
The values

Not everywhere can be protected. So what comes first?

Every sea wall built and every village moved in 2060 draws on the same limited money, land and time. Each of those decisions comes down to three values.

The three values

Efficiency

Making the most of limited money, land and energy by directing them where they achieve the most.

The core question: When resources only go so far, who decides what counts as "the most good", and who is left with less?

Community

The relationships, shared places and sense of belonging that tie people to each other and to where they live.

The core question: What is lost when people are moved for their own protection, and can belonging be rebuilt somewhere new?

Safety

Protecting people from harm, and from the threat of it, as extreme weather becomes more frequent and severe.

The core question: How much money, freedom and familiarity should people give up to be less at risk?

Efficiency and safety

The cheapest plan that protects the most people overall can still leave some places more exposed than others.

Efficiency and community

Moving a small village can cost less than defending it. Its neighbours, halls and history don't move with it.

Community and safety

Staying together in a familiar place and preserving the legacy of your home town can mean putting yourself in harm's way.

There is no right answer, only trade-offs. We want to know how you think society balances them.
